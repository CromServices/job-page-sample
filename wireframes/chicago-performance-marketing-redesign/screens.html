Practical improvements that make the business stronger

Improving your business doesn’t always require more customers, more employees, or a bigger budget. Sometimes, the opportunity is improving how your business already operates. CPM works with business owners to understand what’s affecting performance, identify what needs attention, and help put practical improvements into action.

Sales Performance

More opportunities don’t always mean more sales. Strengthen how your team approaches customers, handles objections, follows up, and converts opportunities into results.

Recruiting & Staffing

Building a strong team takes more than hiring. Improve recruiting, onboarding, training, and employee development to help attract and retain capable employees.

Leadership & Accountability

Unclear expectations and inconsistent accountability can hold teams back. Develop stronger leaders, clarify responsibilities, and build teams that take greater ownership without constant oversight.

Operations & Processes

Even good teams struggle when processes aren’t working. Identify bottlenecks, clarify responsibilities, and improve inconsistent workflows that slow down daily operations.

The goal isn’t to change everything. It’s to identify what needs improvement and focus your efforts where they can make a meaningful difference.

Josh Richards

Founder, Chicago Performance Marketing

A business can have a sales problem that starts with recruiting. A turnover problem that starts with leadership. Or a productivity problem caused by a decision intended to improve efficiency.

For more than 14 years, I’ve worked across sales, recruiting, leadership, and multi-location operations, learning how decisions in one part of a business can affect results in another. My career has taken me from frontline sales to managing teams across multiple markets. Along the way, I’ve recruited and trained employees, developed leaders, rebuilt underperforming teams, and been directly responsible for improving performance.

One of the most important lessons I’ve learned is that better results don’t always come from doing more. Sometimes, they come from understanding what needs to be done differently.

Why I started CPM

Throughout my career, I’ve seen how easily businesses can focus on improving one part of their operation without considering how that decision affects the bigger picture.

One experience involved a team whose employees asked to start their shifts earlier so they could spend more time with their families in the evening. We adjusted their schedules, making their daily routines easier and giving them more time at home. The team responded positively, morale improved, and employees continued exceeding performance expectations. When leadership reviewed the hourly sales numbers, they raised a reasonable concern. The earlier hour wasn’t generating many additional sales, while the later hour appeared to offer stronger sales opportunities. But the team was already performing above expectations, and the earlier schedule had become an important part of what made the arrangement work for them. Rather than looking at the hourly numbers alone, I questioned whether moving the schedule back would actually improve overall performance once employee motivation and the broader impact on the team were considered.

That experience reinforced a principle I’ve carried throughout my career: a better-looking number doesn’t always mean a better-performing business. I founded Chicago Performance Marketing to bring that same practical, broader perspective to other businesses, helping owners look beyond the most obvious problems and understand what may actually be influencing their results.

How I approach business challenges

I don’t believe you can fully understand a business by looking at its numbers alone. Numbers help show what’s happening. Understanding the people, processes, and decisions behind them helps explain why. That’s why I start by listening, asking questions, and learning how the different parts of a business work together before recommending changes.

You know your customers, your employees, and the realities of running your business. I bring an outside perspective shaped by years of hands-on experience managing people, improving processes, and being accountable for results. Together, we can identify what deserves attention, determine which improvements make sense for your business, and explore how CPM can help put those improvements into practice.

What you can expect from me

I won’t recommend a service unless I believe it addresses a meaningful need. I won’t promise results I can’t guarantee. And if I don’t believe CPM is the right fit, I’ll tell you.

My goal isn’t to sell you a solution before understanding your business. It’s to provide practical guidance, reliable support, and work that delivers genuine value. Ultimately, I want business owners to look back on their decision to work with CPM and feel it was an investment worth making.

You know your business. I bring another perspective. Let’s find out what we can improve together.
