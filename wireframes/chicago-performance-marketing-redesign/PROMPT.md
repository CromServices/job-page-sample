## The build

The client asked us to “redesign my company’s website.” “My company, Chicago Performance Marketing (CPM), provides business consulting services focused on improving sales, recruiting, leadership, and overall business performance.” “I’ve already developed much of the website’s content and messaging, but I’d like someone with a strong eye for design who can take what I’ve built and bring it to a more professional level.” “The goal is a clean, modern website that builds credibility, communicates what we offer, and encourages potential clients to reach out.” “The website is currently built using Wix Editor, but I’m open to Wix Studio if there’s a good reason to make the switch.” “I’ll also be recording a short introductory video that I’d like incorporated into the finished website.” “Current website: https://www.chicagoperformancemarketing.com/.” They also asked candidates to “please take a look at the existing website and share a few examples of your work, along with how you would approach the redesign,” and said “I’m happy to discuss the details, timeline, and scope with candidates who seem like a good fit” — those last two belong in the bid letter. ## Key ideas

1. **The first screen is a conversation, not a card grid.** Josh’s headline and the free consultation sit in the same breath, with the Chicago skyline beside them and a reserved typographic place for the forthcoming introductory video. Services come after, as a ruled ledger.
2. **The experience is shown with care.** Live figures (190% YoY, #1, 40+ locations, more than 14 years) and the previous-roles brand marks stay, always with the disclaimer that they reflect Josh’s earlier professional roles, not CPM client engagements or endorsements.
3. **Asking for the conversation is always in reach.** The sticky header, the hero, the three-step close, the booking form, and the footer all lead to the same free 20–30 minute consultation, with the real email and no leftover template contact.

## Emotion of the scope

Client wants customers to feel this looks like a firm they would trust with their business, and that booking a conversation with Josh is the obvious next step.

**Headline feeling: This looks like a firm I would trust with my business, and booking a conversation with Josh feels like the obvious next step.**

### Feeling journey

#### Arrive — sure
- Feeling word: sure
- Inner voice: “This already feels like a serious firm, and those are Josh’s own words.”
- Design levers: hero flush under the CPM monogram, a 2-line H1, the skyline photograph, the consultation invitation, and a reserved place for the video once it is recorded.

#### Explore — clear
- Feeling word: clear
- Inner voice: “They help with sales, recruiting, leadership and operations, in language I would actually use.”
- Design levers: a ruled service ledger in Josh’s language, not a 2×2 card grid.

#### Decide — confident
- Feeling word: confident
- Inner voice: “The experience behind this is real, they are careful about what they claim, and I want to talk it through with the person in the portrait.”
- Design levers: live figures as a ledger, the previous-roles brand marks with the disclaimer in view, and Josh’s portrait and first-person story on Meet the Founder.

#### Act — ready
- Feeling word: ready
- Inner voice: “Twenty to thirty minutes, no cost, no obligation. I’ll ask for the conversation.”
- Design levers: a three-step path, a sticky header consultation button on desktop, the same CTA in the hero and footer, and a booking form with the real follow-up path.

#### After — reassured
- Feeling word: reassured
- Inner voice: “I can picture the conversation, and I can see how they handle my details.”
- Design levers: Privacy Policy and Accessibility Statement in their own words, last updated 17 November 2026, with the real email for contact.

Never feel: a default Wix template; a newly invented brand; serif type; eyebrow labels, slogan headings, card grids or pill badges; grey placeholders or “your photo here”; a dead gap under the nav; leftover `info@mysite.com` / `123-456-7890`; a truncated “Chicago Performanc 3” name; a first screen that only fills a grey canvas with boxed services; or a fake still for a video that has not been recorded yet.

## Standout moment

The opening belongs to Josh’s headline and the consultation invitation in the same breath, with the Chicago skyline beside that message and a reserved typographic place for the introductory video once he records it — not a row of service cards as the first thing you see. On the phone, the skyline sits at the top of the first view, under the monogram and Menu, with “Schedule Your Free Consultation” immediately under the lede.

## Type and colour

- Font: Space Grotesk only (self-hosted). Body 18px desktop / 17px phone. H1 60px desktop (2 lines) / 36px phone (2 lines). 7-step type scale and 4/8 spacing scale in :root.
- Logo: their live CPM monogram (`logo-cpm.png`), 64px desktop / 48px phone, with “Chicago Performance Marketing” typeset beside it on desktop. On the phone the mark stands alone, and the header consultation button is held in the Menu so nothing clips.
- Palette, from their painted live system, without using mid-grey `#cccccc` as the canvas (it reads as a template): ink #000000, soft #2C3338, charcoal footer #252B32, white #FFFFFF, paper #F7F7F5, line #D8D8D4, link #2952D9 (real links only). Black CTAs with white labels. The skyline carries the warmth. No brown or earthy ground, and no invented colour story.
