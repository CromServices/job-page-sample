# Bid wireframe: Build Website from Scratch (Upwork 2122911753703790222)

Quick pre-bid wireframe, 18 Nov 2026, for Director. No business name, sector, logo, colours, copy or photos were supplied. Halden Studio is a clearly labelled sample interior architecture practice. All copy is marked Sample content. Dry-run publish only.

## The build

Quoted from the client post:

- "I need a website built from scratch that is easy to manage and update over time."
- "The site should be clean, professional, and user-friendly, with a strong focus on clear navigation and a polished layout."
- "I will later add blogs and make small changes as needed, so I’m looking for someone who can create a solid foundation that supports future updates."
- "Please share examples of similar projects and your approach to building a site that is both functional and easy to maintain."
- "Can you show me some sites you have already built?"
- "What kind of web design do you really enjoy building?"

## Feeling from the scope

Quoted from the post: "easy to manage and update over time"; "clean, professional, and user-friendly"; "clear navigation and a polished layout"; "solid foundation that supports future updates"; "functional and easy to maintain". A visitor arrives, immediately understands where to go, moves through a calm professional layout without friction, and can reach the business from the nav or the contact page. The owner should feel the site is already theirs to keep — adding a blog post or a small page change later will not mean a redesign.

## Emotion of the scope

Client wants customers to feel: "clean, professional, and user-friendly", that there is "clear navigation and a polished layout", and that the site is "easy to manage and update over time" with a "solid foundation that supports future updates".

**The site already feels like yours to keep.**

### Feeling journey

#### Arrive — oriented
- Feeling word: oriented
- Inner voice: “The path is obvious. This already looks finished.”
- Design levers: hero flush under the nav; Space Grotesk wordmark at a readable height; a 2–3 line H1; one short lede; View services and Read the journal as unclipped buttons; a sunlit room photograph filling the right column.

#### Explore — calm
- Feeling word: calm
- Inner voice: “About, the work, the journal — each sits where it should.”
- Design levers: five-item nav on every screen; three sample services in a tight grid; About as a short overview beside a studio desk; the journal index in a clear reading list; no extra sitemap guessed in.

#### Decide — confident
- Feeling word: confident
- Inner voice: “The practice is clear. Reaching them will be simple.”
- Design levers: service pages with three editable offerings and no invented prices; the journal post template fully worked from a labelled sample entry; typographic hierarchy that stays sharp on a phone.

#### Act — ready
- Feeling word: ready
- Inner voice: “A short form, a clear send. Nothing stands in the way.”
- Design levers: Contact with name, email and message only; 52px buttons that never clip; the same nav and a simple footer on every public page; studio contact shown as sample only.

#### After — kept
- Feeling word: kept
- Inner voice: “A new journal post goes in the same fields, and the home slot already knows.”
- Design levers: Wix Studio hand-off screen; edit a page and press Publish; journal fields for title, tag, date, excerpt, body and cover; a live Home card that updates from those fields; no code.

Never feel: lost in the nav, unsure how to get in touch, stuck in front of an empty picture box, locked out of their own updates, or as if the journal was bolted on later.

## Standout moment

The Home “latest from the journal” slot is already live-wired to the blog CMS. In the wireframe it shows one labelled sample post — “Light, plaster, and a long window”. From that card the visitor opens the post template. On the CMS / hand-off screen the owner sees the matching editor fields; change the title, excerpt, date, tag or cover and the Home slot and the post update together. When the owner adds a real post later in Wix Studio, that same slot updates. That is what “a solid foundation that supports future updates” looks like on the first day.

## Key design ideas

Drawn from the client’s own brief:

1. **Clear navigation, polished layout.** Home, About, Services, Journal and Contact sit in a tight Space Grotesk nav on every screen. The hero meets the nav with no dead band.
2. **A foundation the owner can keep.** Wix Studio is the later build, so pages and journal posts are edited and published without code. The hand-off screen shows that path.
3. **The journal is live from day one.** A sample post fills the template, and the Home latest slot is already wired to the same fields — not an empty blog page waiting for a rebuild.

## Rationale

No client mockup, brand kit or sector was supplied, so the layout is built from the post and fitted to Space Grotesk: modern, edgy, tight rhythm. Halden Studio and all copy are labelled sample content until the owner’s name, logo and words arrive. The later build is Wix Studio only. Examples of similar projects belong in the bid letter, not on the page.
