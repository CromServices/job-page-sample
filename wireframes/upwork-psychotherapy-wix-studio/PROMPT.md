## The build

The client wrote: "I'm an established Australian psychotherapist looking for a talented Wix Studio designer to elevate the visual identity, design and user experience of my professional practice website." They have "an existing live website and a partially developed version in Wix Studio." "Much of the website copy is already written, and I have an established colour direction. However, the branding, typography, logo and overall visual identity are not yet finalised." The site should feel "Premium and understated", "Warm, calm and spacious", "Grounded and authentic", "Professional and trustworthy", "Contemporary and sophisticated", and "Quietly confident." They want to "avoid generic wellness aesthetics, overly spiritual imagery, corporate styling, excessive animations and unnecessary visual clutter." Scope includes refining visual identity, typography, colours and imagery, "a simple logo or wordmark if appropriate", layouts, "the homepage and existing service pages", "navigation, user experience and enquiry pathways", responsiveness, and implementation in Wix Studio. ## Tonal register

**Quiet / still.** Unhurried rooms, a long measure of type, very little motion. A professional practice, not a wellness brand and not a clinic template.

- Motion: almost still. The appointment walk and the enquiry reply fade in over about 240 ms. Nothing loops. Everything is already complete under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Type: Plus Jakarta Sans (sans only, self-hosted, one variable file). H1 56px desktop / 36px phone, semi-bold, tight tracking. The enquiry heading 56px / 36px. Body 18px / 17px.
- Colour and imagery: a concept direction, not presented as theirs. Luminous paper `#F5F7FB`, wall `#E8EDF6`, ink `#161C2C`, quiet blue `#2C4A8C`, muted `#3A4458`, line `#C5CEE0`, sky `#D9E4F7`, a cool rose `#B54A6C` used only as a thin accent. No browns, rust, tan, sand, ochre or beige. Imagery is one matched generated set from a single reference: a cool, spare consulting room in daylight, the same blue-grey chair, the same doorway, the same window, and a writing table. No people, no text in the photographs, no spiritual or spa props.
- Copy: a practice speaking plainly to someone who is considering an enquiry. Short sentences, Australian English, no slogans, no results language.

Skeleton: the threshold aperture. A continuous wall plane. The first viewport is a deep-set doorway: the photograph is seen through lintel and jambs, and the headline sits on the wall beneath the sill, left-locked, never over the picture and never in a second column. Later photographs are further punched openings in the same wall, not full-bleed centred bands. The enquiry is a writing table under a window in that architecture, at the same energy as the doorway. Not a two-column headline-and-photo, not a diptych, not an atelier rail, not a browser window, not a photo with a docked console and overlapping sheets, not an eave with a hanging docket, and not phone frames on a spec board.

## Key ideas

1. **The doorway is the hook.** The first thing you see is a quiet room, through a threshold, and a sentence that already points to writing in.
2. **One appointment, not a menu.** A single service page about coming in, with room for their already-written copy, and a short walk through what to expect.
3. **The enquiry is a note on a table.** Not a booking engine. Name, email, a note, and a clear reply.

## Emotion of the scope

Client wants customers to feel they have found a calm, considered practice, and that writing in is the natural next step.

**Headline feeling: I have found the right room, and I can leave a note.**

**Sell / hook**
- Desire: someone considering this practice wants to feel it is a serious, calm place, and to know how to get in touch without hunting.
- Hook: in the first viewport, a doorway into a quiet room, a 56px sentence on the wall, and Enquire already in reach.
- Objection killers: "Is this a generic wellness site?" (architectural rooms, no spiritual props, no card grid); "Will this invent a brand they did not ask for?" (no practice name, typography and an aperture mark only, colour left open for theirs); "Can people actually get in touch?" (a short written enquiry, working, with a plain reply).
- Proof: the client's own brief — an established Australian practice, copy and a colour direction already in hand, a partially developed Wix Studio site, and a later paid trial on the first homepage sections.
- The one conversion action: Enquire. It sits in the header, under the doorway, at the end of the appointment walk, and as the form itself.

### Feeling journey

#### Arrive — still
- Feeling word: still
- Inner voice: “This room has space for me.”
- Design levers: a left-locked doorway aperture, a 56px sentence on the wall, generous wall around the opening, Enquire close at hand.

#### Explore — considered
- Feeling word: considered
- Inner voice: “The work is clear, and nothing is being sold to me.”
- Design levers: a long typographic measure, a second aperture of the same chair and window, short standing copy with room for theirs.

#### Decide — sure
- Feeling word: sure
- Inner voice: “This is a serious practice, and I would be all right writing to them.”
- Design levers: one appointment page, no service cards; a three-step walk through what to expect; the chair in daylight.

#### Act — ready
- Feeling word: ready
- Inner voice: “I’ll leave a note. That’s enough for now.”
- Design levers: a writing table under the window, a 56px heading, a short form, a send control large enough to tap.

#### After — held
- Feeling word: held
- Inner voice: “They have what I wrote. I can close the page.”
- Design levers: a quiet closing plane that states a reply will come to the address they gave.

Never feel: a wellness brand, spiritual imagery, a spa, a hospital portal, a corporate template, a card grid of services, serif type, eyebrow labels, slogan headings, pill badges, earth colours, invented credentials, or any promise about outcomes.

## Standout moment

The threshold. You arrive at a doorway and see the room before you read a word. The same care returns at the writing table, where leaving a note is as clear as walking in.

## Working interactions

1. **Navigation.** Practice, The appointment and Enquire move to those sections, mark the current place, and open a menu on a phone.
2. **Appointment walk.** Three moments — before you write, when you come in, after you write — advanced with Next and Back.
3. **Enquiry form (the Act moment).** Name, email and a note; errors in words; Send shows a quiet thank-you.
