## The build

The client asked for "a premium, elegant design using navy blue, gold, and white", and for the site to be bilingual, mobile-friendly, and to include services, About Us, portfolio, a WhatsApp contact button and an email enquiry form. They also asked for "no mandatory monthly hosting or website-builder fees" and full ownership. This wireframe covers that screen set: one long Home in English, the same Home in Turkish, phone views of both, and a short hand-off view. There is no shop, booking, blog, login or chatbot build.

## Key ideas

1. **A bilingual site that switches in place.** English and Turkish are a craft, not a toggle buried in a footer: one gold underline slides from EN to TR, the whole page changes language, and the scroll position stays put.
2. **The chatbot is shown, not claimed.** In the hero, a short written exchange between a café owner and AT Precious Tech's assistant takes a late booking and leaves it for the morning, in the language the customer uses.
3. **The finished work is handed over.** The hand-off view says only this: your code, your hosting account, your domain — no monthly hosting or builder fee; you renew your domain.

## Emotion of the scope

Client wants customers to feel they have found a calm, senior team that speaks their language, explains technology plainly, and hands them something they fully own. The client's own words are "modern, professional" and "premium, elegant". The ask for full ownership and no mandatory monthly fees is the wish to feel in control, not locked in.

**Headline feeling: After hours, still answered in my language — and the site stays mine.**

### Feeling journey

#### Arrive — understood
- Feeling word: understood
- Inner voice: “They already know the 11pm call is the one I miss, and they can say it in my language.”
- Design levers: the hero sits flush under the wordmark, a three-line headline over a real photograph of a café owner on the phone, and the café-owner ↔ assistant exchange just below, with the gold EN/TR underline in the header.

#### Explore — oriented
- Feeling word: oriented
- Inner voice: “Website, AI, chatbots, automation — each one said in their words, and what actually changes for my customers is right there.”
- Design levers: services as a navy ledger of four full-width rows and gold hairlines, not a card grid; About Us in plain prose beside a founder at a laptop, then a bilingual site on an open laptop.

#### Decide — sure
- Feeling word: sure
- Inner voice: “That café got the after-hours bookings, and the site ended up theirs.”
- Design levers: one featured project at full width — Calder Moss Café, three sentences and one real-looking screen — then a quiet list of name, sector and one line.

#### Act — ready
- Feeling word: ready
- Inner voice: “English or Turkish is fine — or WhatsApp is already in the header.”
- Design levers: a short enquiry form (name, email, service, message) next to a WhatsApp action; the same WhatsApp control stays in the header on every view.

#### After — in-control
- Feeling word: in-control
- Inner voice: “The code is mine, the hosting account is mine, the domain is mine. I’m not paying a builder every month.”
- Design levers: a short navy hand-off page, the two ownership lines only, nothing more specific about prices.

Never feel: flashy, salesy, robotic, cheap, or “AI hype”. No robot hands, glowing brains, circuit boards, or slogan pairs.

### Standout moment

In the hero, a café owner asks how to take bookings after closing. AT Precious Tech's assistant answers in two plain lines. When the visitor taps TR, that exchange and the whole page switch to Turkish in place, the scroll position is kept, and a gold underline slides from EN to TR. Bilingual reads as craft, and the chatbot service is shown, not claimed. The exchange is static text; there is no working chatbot in the wireframe.
