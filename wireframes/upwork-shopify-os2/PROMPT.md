## The build

The client wrote they want an expert to "engineer a high-converting, ultra-fast D2C e-commerce store completely from scratch using Shopify Online Store 2.0 and custom Liquid coding." They "strictly do NOT want bloated drag-and-drop page builders (like PageFly, Shogun, or GemPages) or dozens of monthly third-party apps that kill site speed." Deliverables include a "custom, mobile-first Header with a lightweight coded Mega Menu and predictive search bar", a "Custom AJAX Slide-Out Cart Drawer" with a "Free Shipping Progress Bar" and "native cart upsell/cross-sell", and work "aligned with our luxury D2C brand guidelines." They aim "to achieve 90+ Google PageSpeed Insights scores on Mobile and Desktop." Brand guidelines, a business name and a current-store URL were not supplied. Full checklist in COVERAGE.md.

## Feeling from the scope

"a luxury D2C store that feels fast the moment it opens, and buying from it feels simple."

## Tonal register

**Luxe, on warm paper.** A visitor should feel they have walked into a finished luxury storefront that is already quiet and light, in the first five seconds.

- Motion: slow and short. Colour changes fade once; the bag eases in and stops; the shipping remainder settles. No bounce, no loops. Everything is already complete under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Type: Instrument Sans (a quieter, more material sans than Space Grotesk), tight tracking on the wordmark, tabular numerals for price and the shipping remainder. The H1 is 44px, restrained for luxe; the shipping remainder in `#bag` is 88px, the one large number in the store.
- Colour and imagery: warm ivory paper `#FFF6EC`, ink `#161414`, one lacquer red `#B11212`. Photography is one matched set of a wool overcoat (ivory, ink, lacquer, a three-quarter view, a fabric close-up) on the same warm ground. No grey, no cool metal, no earthy brown field.
- Copy: the store talking quietly about the garment and the theme, using the client's phrases where they help, never a slogan.

Skeleton: an atelier rail. A slim vertical typographic spine holds the word "Concept" and the floor list; the merchandising sits on a right-bleeding plinth, with a short type column overlapping it. Not a two-column headline-and-photo, not a diptych, and not a centred stack of full-bleed bands.

## Key ideas

1. **The store is already finished, and already light.** The first screen is a single coat on a warm plinth, under a working header, not a theme demo.
2. **Colour, search and the bag are native to the theme.** Swatches, predictive search and a hand-coded bag replace the app stack the client does not want.
3. **Opening the bag is an event, not a utility panel.** The Act stage is a lacquer field with a giant free-shipping remainder, live quantities and one native upsell.

## Emotion of the scope

Client wants customers to feel a luxury D2C store that is already fast the moment it opens, and that buying from it is simple.

**Headline feeling: This already feels like a finished luxury storefront, and the next step is obvious.**

**Sell / hook**
- Desire: the shopper wants the coat, quickly, from a store that feels considered. The client wants that store built from scratch in Liquid, without page builders or a stack of apps.
- Hook: in the first viewport the ivory coat is already on the plinth, flush under a working header. Changing Ivory, Ink or Lacquer swaps the photograph; typing in search finds the coat, the fabric guide or an order. Both are usable from the first screen.
- Objection killers: "Will it feel like a page builder?" (the store is one typographic spine and a plinth, with no app chrome); "Will the cart feel bolted on?" (the bag is a full lacquer stage with live quantities and a shipping remainder); "Will it work on a phone?" (a mobile-first header, a sticky add bar on the product, the same plinth reflowed).
- Proof: the interactions themselves, plus a page that stays light (one self-hosted font, lazy images below the fold, a small phone first view). Prices, delivery days and the shipping threshold are concept figures, not the client's.
- The one conversion action: add to bag, then checkout from the bag.

### Feeling journey

#### Arrive — assured
- Feeling word: assured
- Inner voice: “This already looks like a real store. And I can change the colour now.”
- Design levers: ivory coat on a right-bleeding plinth, 44px H1 in two lines, three colour swatches that swap the photograph, one Add to bag button, the header already offering Shop and Search.

#### Explore — unhurried
- Feeling word: unhurried
- Inner voice: “I can move through the colourways without the page getting heavier.”
- Design levers: a horizontal rack of the same coat in three colours, typographic filters for price, availability, colour and category, a load-more that reveals the third colourway.

#### Decide — certain
- Feeling word: certain
- Inner voice: “I know the cloth, I know the size, I know when it arrives.”
- Design levers: the coat large on the plinth, image swatches, an interactive size chart, a fabric guide with the cloth close-up, metafield specs, a typographic delivery estimate, a sticky Add to bag on the phone.

#### Act — ready
- Feeling word: ready
- Inner voice: “Eighty dollars to free shipping. I’ll take the other colour too.”
- Design levers: a full lacquer stage; an 88px typographic remainder; a progress rule that fills; live quantity; one native upsell (the ink colourway) as a single object, not a list; checkout.

#### After — light
- Feeling word: light
- Inner voice: “The store is still quiet. I can see where the order is.”
- Design levers: a large typographic tracking path (Paid, Cut, Packed, Shipped) that answers to a concept order number, a short closing note, and the concept annotation.

Never feel: PageFly / Shogun / GemPages heaviness; app-stacked clutter; a generic Dawn-unchanged starter; a made-up company presented as the real client; serif type; eyebrow labels, slogan headings, card grids or pill badges; grey placeholders or "your photo here"; a dead gap under the nav; or any section that falls back to a plain template band or list.

## Standout moment

The bag. Opening it should feel like part of the store: a lacquer field, a giant free-shipping remainder, live quantities and one native upsell, while the rest of the store stays quiet and fast. The first screen earns that moment by already feeling finished.

## Working interactions

1. **Colour swatches (first viewport).** Ivory, Ink and Lacquer swap the hero photograph. Keyboard operable. Instant under reduced motion.
2. **Predictive search (first viewport).** Typing filters a short typographic list (the coat, fabric, tracking) and jumps to the section.
3. **Mega menu.** Shop opens a typographic panel of the five surfaces, not a product card grid.
4. **Collection filters and load-more.** Facets for price, availability, colour and category hide and show colourways without a reload; load-more reveals the third coat.
5. **Size chart and fabric guide on the product.** Choosing a size highlights the measurements; opening the fabric guide shows the cloth close-up and the metafield copy.
6. **The bag (the Act moment).** Add to bag, change quantity, watch the free-shipping remainder and bar update, add the ink upsell, or open the same stage from the header. The phone product page also has a sticky Add to bag.

## Type and colour

- Font: Instrument Sans only (self-hosted variable). Body 18px desktop / 17px phone. H1 44px desktop (2 lines) / 32px phone. Shipping remainder 88px. 7-step type scale and 4/8 spacing scale in :root.
- Logo: a typeset "C" in a 64px (desktop) / 48px (phone) square, with the word "Concept" beside it on desktop only.
- Palette: ink `#161414`, paper `#FFF6EC`, lacquer `#B11212`, cream text on lacquer, muted `#3F3C3A`.
