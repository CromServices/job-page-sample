## Tonal register

**Calm / methodical.** A visitor should feel carefully looked after in the first five seconds: nothing is being thrown away, everything is being put in order.

- Motion: slow and almost still. The divider sweeps once over about 1.6 seconds and stops; the approval count settles; the confirmation fades in. No bounce, no loops. Everything is already complete under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Type: our framing chrome is Instrument Sans (sans only), tight tracking, tabular numerals. Inside the browser window, the site keeps its own Roboto and Sora, so the After view reads as their site and not ours. H1 44px; the approval count 96px.
- Colour and imagery: warm paper `#FFF6EC`, ink `#161414`, one warm amber `#F4A12A` for toggles and the divider handle. The window shows their real navy `#0C4A74` and blue `#0071BC`. Imagery is only what their site already shows: the Brisbane skyline, the factory shot, S-Gate Valve, Hydra-Cut and the utility photo, as crops of the captures. No stock, no invented photos.
- Copy: a careful tradesperson talking plainly about what we found and what we will touch first. Plain words, no technical terms.

Skeleton: stage-first. A full-width browser window carries their real site in the first viewport, with a slim row of controls above it. Below it, a tab drawer for the triage with the tabs docked on the left, a theme-and-promises strip, and a full-bleed approval board. Not a two-column headline-and-photo, not a diptych, not a rail, not a centred stack of bands.

## Key ideas

1. **Same site, side by side.** Their real captured homepage and the fixed version share one window with a draggable divider. Drag, or use Before and After.
2. **Say what we found, in plain words.** Three tabs: what we found, what we fix first and why, what we flag for later. Every item is something visible on their public pages.
3. **Nothing goes without a yes.** The final step is an approval board. Each fix has Approve or Hold, the count updates, and the confirmation says nothing is hidden or removed until they say so.

## Emotion of the scope

Client wants customers to feel that's our site, just fixed.

**Headline feeling: This is our site, just fixed, and nothing has been taken away.**

**Sell / hook**
- Desire: the client wants the current site to look credible and accurate while the larger rebuild is planned, without losing what already works.
- Hook: in the first viewport, their real homepage and the fixed one share one browser window; the divider sweeps once, and the hero line, the products and the footer change under the visitor's finger.
- Objection killers: "Will it still look like us?" (same blue, type, logo and menu; `#keep` shows their real values); "Will anything be deleted by mistake?" (a backup first, a list sent first, an approval board with Hold on every line); "Will it work on a phone?" (the whole window works at 390, and their current mobile page is the Before).
- Proof: what is actually on their site today: a Privacy Policy link that goes nowhere on every page, S-Gate Valve missing from the homepage body, documents labelled "Click for CNS Engineering Australia", PDFs of 5.0 and 9.5 MB. All checked on the public pages on 23 Nov 2026.
- The one conversion action: confirm the fix list (the approval board).

### Feeling journey

#### Arrive — recognised
- Feeling word: recognised
- Inner voice: “That’s our homepage. And there’s the same page next to it, fixed.”
- Design levers: a full-width browser window with their real header, a divider that sweeps once, Before and After buttons, a 44px two-line H1 flush under the controls.

#### Explore — clear
- Feeling word: clear
- Inner voice: “I can see exactly what is wrong, in words I use.”
- Design levers: three tabs docked on the left (what we found, what we fix first, what we flag), short plain sentences, each tied to a page the visitor can open.

#### Decide — reassured
- Feeling word: reassured
- Inner voice: “They are keeping our design and our wording. I can see our own blue and logo.”
- Design levers: their real logo, navy and blue swatches with the values read from the site, their menu, and a plain order of work: backup, list, your yes, changes, checks.

#### Act — in-control
- Feeling word: in-control
- Inner voice: “Five approved, three held. Nothing happens until I say.”
- Design levers: a full-bleed navy board; a 96px tabular count of approvals; eight rows each with Approve and Hold; a confirm button; a confirmation that fades in.

#### After — credible
- Feeling word: credible
- Inner voice: “This is the site I would be happy to send a customer to.”
- Design levers: a full-bleed amber closing line, “The site you would be happy to send a customer to.”, with buttons that return to the Before or the After at the top.

Never feel: a redesign, a different company's site, a developer's template, technical jargon, anything removed without asking, serif type (in our chrome), eyebrow labels, slogan headings, card grids or pill badges, grey placeholders or "your photo here", a dead gap under the nav, or any section that falls back to a plain band or list.

## Standout moment

The divider. On load it sweeps once from their real page to the fixed page and stops in the middle, with the visitor holding the handle. The same attention returns in the approval board, where the big count follows each decision.

## Working interactions

1. **Before / After divider (first viewport).** Drag, use the slider under it or the arrow keys, or press Before / After / Compare. Sweeps once on load; fixed at the middle under reduced motion.
2. **Triage tabs.** Found, Fix first, Flag for later; arrow keys move between tabs.
3. **Approval board (the Act moment).** Approve or Hold per fix; a rolling count; confirm; a confirmation that fades in.
4. **Jump links.** Technology, Documents and Enquiry scroll both pages to that part, and ease the divider to show more of the After.

## Type and colour

- Chrome font: Instrument Sans only (self-hosted). Body 18px desktop / 17px phone. H1 44px / 32px. Approval count 96px / 64px. 7-step type scale and 4/8 spacing scale in :root.
- Inside the window: Roboto and Sora, as the live site, self-hosted, so the After view matches.
- Logo: their real logo from the capture, 64px high desktop / 48px phone in the window header.
- Palette (chrome): paper `#FFF6EC`, ink `#161414`, amber `#F4A12A`, muted `#3F3C3A`. Inside the window: their `#0C4A74`, `#0071BC`, white and black.
