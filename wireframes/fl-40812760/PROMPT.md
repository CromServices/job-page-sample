# Bid wireframe: contractor site build and Google Workspace migration (Freelancer 40812760)

Quick bid wireframe, 15 Nov 2026, for Director. Built from the portfolio (nearest citable row: **general-power-wix**, https://www.generalpower.com.au — a Wix trades rebuild, not WordPress or Webflow; cite only if Director is happy). Not a full build. Business name, trade and current domain are not in the post. Do not publish.

## The build

Two parallel tasks, quoted from the client post: "a clean, modern website that highlights my completed projects and service offerings," and "a full move from my legacy business email system to Google Workspace."

Website:

- "The site’s sole purpose is to showcase past work and clearly outline the services I provide."
- "a straightforward portfolio layout with space for project photos, brief case-study style descriptions, and an easy-to-find contact form"
- "It must load quickly, look polished on mobile, and be simple for me to update"
- "WordPress, Webflow, or a similarly intuitive CMS is fine as long as I can add new projects myself after hand-over."

Email and DNS:

- "transferred to Google Workspace with all existing messages, folders, and aliases intact"
- "DNS records will also need updating so the new domain-based accounts send and receive flawlessly"

Deliverables:

- "Fully functional website published on my domain, with CMS access handed over"
- "All existing email data migrated to Google Workspace, tested and verified"
- "Updated DNS settings for both web and mail, plus a brief hand-off guide"

They will evaluate "a detailed project proposal that outlines your approach, timeline, and any tool stack" — "the proposal itself is the key item I will evaluate."

## Feeling from the scope

Quoted from the post: "a clean, modern website" that must "look polished on mobile" and "load quickly". The work itself is the hero: "showcase past work". For the owner, the email move must happen with "all existing messages, folders, and aliases intact" and accounts that "send and receive flawlessly" — nothing lost.

## Concept

The finished work sells the business. A split hero puts a completed kitchen beside one plain promise. The project grid invites close looking; each case study shows before and after, the brief, the work and the timeline. Services are said in plain words. The contact form is short and always one tap away. For the owner, two panels show the CMS "add a project" screen and the go-live plan for the website, Google Workspace and DNS.

## Emotion of the scope

Client wants customers to feel: they are looking at "a clean, modern website" that is "polished on mobile", where "completed projects" show the quality before a word is said, and the "easy-to-find contact form" is right there. (Quoted from the client post.)

**Confidence: this builder’s work speaks before they do.**

Not a task. The moment a homeowner looks at a finished room and thinks, mine could look like that.

### Feeling journey

#### Arrive — impressed
- Feeling word: impressed
- Inner voice: “That marble, that light. Somebody took real care here.”
- Design levers: split hero with a finished kitchen at full height, flush under the dark nav; Manrope 800 H1 over two to three lines; one clear quote button in the nav and one in the hero; fast first view.

#### Explore — trust
- Feeling word: trust
- Inner voice: “Before and after, side by side. The cabinets line up. They finished the job.”
- Design levers: project grid with 4:3 photographs and short titles; category chips; case study with before/after labels, brief, work done and timeline; honest, short copy.

#### Decide — conviction
- Feeling word: conviction
- Inner voice: “Kitchens and extensions, start to finish, one person to call. That’s us.”
- Design levers: three services in plain words with photographs of the crew at work; no jargon; a quote button after the case study.

#### Act — ease
- Feeling word: ease
- Inner voice: “Four boxes and send. No hunting for a phone number.”
- Design levers: four-field contact form; job-type select; 50px unclipped button; reply promise in plain words.

#### After — assured
- Feeling word: assured
- Inner voice: “My emails, my folders, my new projects, all where they should be.”
- Design levers: CMS screen with "Publish project"; a three-step go-live plan for the site, Google Workspace and DNS; dark calm band with no technical clutter.

Never feel:

- That the photos are stock or staged
- Lost looking for the contact form
- Talked down to with trade jargon
- Worried the site will be hard to update
- Afraid of losing old emails in the move

## Rationale

The client says the proposal is what they will judge. The wireframe makes the proposal concrete: it shows the site they described, the screen they will use to add projects, and the order in which the website, mail and DNS move so that nothing is lost. Projects, services and timelines are labelled "Sample content" until their own photos and words are supplied.
